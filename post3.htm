5 PC Maintenance Tips Every School Lab Needs

📁 IT & Systems 📅 August 3, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

School computer labs are notorious for slow, unreliable machines. After 18 years in Ethiopian schools – and running my own PC repair service – I've seen it all. Dust-clogged fans, bloated hard drives, and labs that lose an entire week of teaching to downtime.

Here are five simple, free maintenance habits that will keep your school lab running smoothly. No expensive tools required – just consistency.

1. Schedule a Monthly Disk Cleanup

The problem: Students download files, install random software, and leave temporary files everywhere. Within a month, a 256GB drive can be 80% full – and Windows slows to a crawl.

The fix: Use the built-in Disk Cleanup tool (search for it in the Start menu). Run it on every lab PC at the end of each month. Check the boxes for Temporary files, Recycle Bin, and Downloads folder if it's safe.

🛠 Pro tip: Create a batch script that runs Disk Cleanup automatically and schedule it via Task Scheduler. One command, zero manual effort.

2. Dust Out the Fans Every Term

The problem: Ethiopian classrooms are dusty. Labs with open windows or poor sealing get clogged fans, causing overheating, random shutdowns, and permanent hardware damage.

The fix: Once per school term, turn off each PC, open the case (or use compressed air through vents), and blow out dust from fans and heat sinks. Desktops need this more than laptops, but both benefit.

Safety note: Hold fans still when blowing air – spinning them too fast can damage bearings.

3. Uninstall Bloatware and Old Software

The problem: PCs come with pre-installed trial software, toolbars, and unnecessary apps that eat memory and CPU in the background.

The fix: Use Settings > Apps > Installed Apps to review and remove anything that isn't needed for teaching. Focus on:

  • Duplicate utilities (e.g., multiple PDF readers).
  • Manufacturer trial software (antivirus trials, office trials).
  • Old versions of software after updates.

4. Set Up Guest Accounts with Restrictions

The problem: Students install games, change settings, or accidentally delete system files when given full user accounts.

The fix: Create a standard (non-admin) user account for students. In Windows, go to Settings > Accounts > Other users and add a new user without administrator privileges. Use that account for all student logins.

Bonus: Enable Windows' "Assigned Access" (if you have Pro version) to lock PCs to a single educational app or browser.

5. Run a Weekly Malware Scan

The problem: Students plug in USB drives, visit unsafe websites, and download "free" software that comes with hidden malware.

The fix: Use Windows Security (built-in) – it's free and effective. Schedule a full scan every Sunday night. In Virus & threat protection, click Scan options and choose Full scan.

If you want extra protection, install Malwarebytes Free and run it monthly as a second opinion.

📋 Quick Checklist for Lab Managers:
  • ✅ Disk Cleanup – Monthly
  • ✅ Dust fans – Every term
  • ✅ Uninstall bloatware – Once per term
  • ✅ Guest accounts – Set up once, use always
  • ✅ Malware scan – Weekly

Final Thought

You don't need a dedicated IT team to keep a school lab healthy. These five habits take less than 30 minutes per PC per term – and they prevent 80% of common failures. Small effort, huge impact on teaching time.
