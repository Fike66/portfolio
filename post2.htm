Why I Taught Physics for 18 Years – and Now Code

📁 Personal Story 📅 June 15, 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

I never planned to become a programmer. My first love was physics – the elegant laws that govern the universe. I spent years teaching physics at different secondary schools in urban and rural Ethiopian communities.

But after 16 years of teaching, I noticed something:

  • Teachers were drowning in administrative paperwork.
  • Students needed more personalized feedback than handwritten reports could provide.
  • Schools lacked affordable, simple software to automate repetitive tasks.
💡 The Turning Point: I realized that my physics training – logical thinking, problem decomposition, systems analysis – was actually perfect preparation for coding. So I decided to learn programming to solve the problems I saw every day.

From Physics to Programming

I started with Excel automation, HTML and JavaScript. My first project was a simple grade calculator. Then I built a student report generator and statistical data reports. Then a PowerShell toolkit for system maintenance. Each project taught me something new, and each one solved a real need for my school and other nearby schools.

What Physics Taught Me About Code

  • Abstraction: Just like modeling a physical system, you break problems into smaller parts.
  • Precision: A missing decimal point in physics changes everything – same with code.
  • Experimentation: You test hypotheses (debugging), observe results (outputs), and iterate.

Why I'm Still a Teacher at Heart

Even though I code now, I haven't left teaching behind. I'm simply using technology as a new tool. The goal is still the same: to help students, educators and schools succeed. Whether I'm explaining physics concepts or automating a report card, I'm solving problems and empowering people.

My journey from physics to programming wasn't a career change – it was a skill expansion.
